Berryessa & Alum Rock, San José · Updated

The trail along
Penitencia Creek

Penitencia Creek tumbles out of Alum Rock Park and runs west to Coyote Creek. Beside it, a trail links San José's oldest park to a BART station: the first direct transit connection in the Bay Area Ridge Trail's history. Both ends are still a few hundred yards short.

Meet the trail

From the train to the canyon

The Penitencia Creek Trail is a chain of short pieces built by the City, the County and Valley Water since the 1970s: along Mabury Rd, through Penitencia Creek County Park, past the percolation ponds, and on boardwalks into the foothills. Since 2020 it starts at the Berryessa/North San José BART station.

About 4.2 miles are open, from BART to Dorel Dr, roughly 0.9 of them gravel.

Two short pieces are missing: 0.5 mile from BART across the flea market to Coyote Creek, which waits on Valley Water's flood project, and 0.17 mile from Dorel Dr to the Alum Rock Park gate, which needs an easement a neighbor wouldn't give.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      Most of the trail was built cheaply on water-district and county land in the 1990s and 2000s. The recent pieces cost far more per mile, and the missing link to Coyote Creek is tied to a flood project with no construction money.

      Project table with contracts and funding sources

      Promises and what happened

      Reach 1B took three bid rounds and came in 59% over its 2013 budget. The BART link opened two years late, because BART did. The flood project that would finish the trail lost its construction money in 2023.

      Valley Water's flood project, phase by phase

      The trail's missing half mile to Coyote Creek is part of Phase I. Valley Water picked this plan in 2019 at $24M for Phases I–II (2019 dollars), then removed the construction money in January 2023 and is now re-planning it for targeted fixes.
