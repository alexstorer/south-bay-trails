Evergreen, San José · Updated

Seven miles planned,
under two paved

San José planned a 7-mile trail up Thompson Creek in 2005, from Lake Cunningham to the Evergreen foothills. A gravel path opened in 2008. Paving came a mile at a time, in 2017 and 2023. The other six miles have a place on a grant list and no money.

Meet the trail

A creek trail built a mile at a time

Thompson Creek drains Evergreen's foothills north to Lake Cunningham, mostly in a Valley Water flood channel. The City's 2005 master plan put a trail of about 7 miles beside it, from Tully Rd to Heartland Way at San Felipe Rd, and a gravel trail opened from Tully Rd to Aborn Rd in 2008.

About 1.7 miles are paved: Tully Rd to Quimby Rd in 2017, and Quimby Rd to Aborn Ct in 2023 with a VTA Measure B grant. A 0.9-mile gravel path on the east bank makes a loop at the north end.

The remaining 6.2 miles, south from Aborn Rd, are unfunded. The City asked VTA for $2.41M to design them in 2020; they're on VTA's 10-year list, still waiting.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      The two paved stretches were budgeted at a few million dollars between them, but the City never published a final cost for either. The six miles to the south have one City estimate, from 2019, and a grant request that VTA listed but hasn't funded.

      Project table with funding sources

      Promises and what happened

      Tully–Quimby finished about nine months after its first published date. Quimby–Aborn opened on time once money arrived, but its plans had waited six years. No contract amounts, bids or final costs turned up for either.

      The City's own numbers

      The 2005 master plan, and any estimate in it, is no longer online. Since then the City has priced the trail only in passing: $13.0M in 2019 for Quimby Rd to the city limit, and a $2.41M Measure B request in 2020, of which $610,000 was awarded.

      Estimate the cost of closing the gaps
