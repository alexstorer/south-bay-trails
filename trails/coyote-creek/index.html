The trail along
Coyote Creek

A ribbon of path follows Coyote Creek for about 30 miles, from the salt marshes at the edge of San Francisco Bay, through the heart of San José, out to the oak hills below Anderson Dam. Most of it is open. A few hard miles still are not.

Meet the trail

A creek you can follow almost all the way

Coyote Creek starts in the Diablo Range, fills Anderson Reservoir, and winds north through San José to the Bay. Beside it runs one of the Bay Area's great urban trails. Parts of it belong to the Bay Area Ridge Trail and to a designated National Recreation Trail.

On a single path you can pass Happy Hollow Park & Zoo, Kelley Park and Hellyer County Park, then ride out to the percolation ponds of Coyote Valley. Today about 21 miles connect without a break, from William Street Park near downtown south to Anderson Dam.

North of downtown, the trail breaks into short, disconnected pieces. Between Montague Expressway and William Street, about 4.9 miles still need bridges, undercrossings and land agreements before East San José and Berryessa can ride the creek all the way to the Bay Trail.

      Did they deliver? On time and on budget

      For each project, the finish date the City planned when it awarded the contract, compared with when the trail actually opened. Every project since 2021 has been finished, each one a few months late. Final closeout costs are rarely published. The one project with a reported final number, Mabury–Empire, went over budget.

      The City's own estimate, from 2011

      The 2011 master plan for the northern gap, Montague Expwy to Watson Park, priced each reach in 2010 dollars. The figures cover construction only, with no design, contingency or inflation. The one piece built since then is Mabury–Empire, inside Reach 4. Its construction contract was $7.5M for 0.58 mi, about 2× the plan's per-mile figure for that reach, and the total project cost came to $13.7M.

      Estimate the cost of closing the gaps

      The City hasn't updated its 2011 estimate. Its current figure covers only the whole 67-mile trail network: $300–400 million, with "no defined funding." This calculator applies a cost-per-mile assumption, in today's total project dollars, to the remaining mainline gaps.

        About this page

        This explorer brings together public records: City of San José Council memos and GIS data, Valley Water and County Parks releases, State Coastal Conservancy staff reports, and local journalism. Each event and figure links to its source.

        It's an independent project and is not affiliated with the City of San José, Valley Water, or Santa Clara County.

        • Reach lengths differ between sources. Mabury–Empire is reported as both 0.3 and 0.58 mi.
        • The GIS status can lag real-world closures. For example, reaches near the Senter Rd trestle show as open after the 2023 closure.
        • Total costs include design and staff time. Contract amounts are listed separately in the table.
        • The gap-cost range is our extrapolation, not an official City estimate.
