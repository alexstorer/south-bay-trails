The trail along
the old rail line

For seventy years, Western Pacific trains ran behind Five Wounds Church and over Coyote Creek on a timber trestle. The trains stopped around 1995, and by 2002 neighbors had made a trail on the tracks a top priority. Today a widened sidewalk is all that's open.

Meet the trail

Two creeks, one rail line, one BART station

The Five Wounds Trail would turn the abandoned San Jose Branch of the Western Pacific into a walking and biking path through Little Portugal and Five Wounds/Brookwood Terrace. It would link the Lower Silver Creek Trail in the north to the Coyote Creek Trail in the south, and give East San José a car-free way to the future 28th St/Little Portugal BART station.

Today only 0.67 miles are open, and they aren't on the railbed: a widened sidewalk along 22nd St, built by a housing developer around 2006.

The other 1.7 miles wait on three things: soil cleanup and a land transfer from VTA in the middle, BART construction in the north, and a decision about the 1922 trestle over Coyote Creek in the south.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      No public money has built any trail here yet. What has been spent has gone to studies and plans. The City's own construction estimates, from two unsuccessful state grant bids, run about $34 million.

      Table of spending with funding sources

      Promises and what happened

      The City has set dates for this trail several times. Each has passed or been abandoned: construction by 2025, then by 2026, then an opening by 2031.

      Three ways across Coyote Creek

      In 2023 the City's engineers priced three options for the trestle, construction only. All three cost about the same, $6.9–7.7 million, so the choice comes down to history, permits and upkeep. Keeping the trestle and building a new bridge beside it is cheapest to build but carries the most staff cost. Replacing it would need a full EIR, because the trestle appears eligible for the National Register.

        About this page

        This explorer brings together public records: San José Council memos and GIS data, the 2023 trestle studies, VTA and State Coastal Conservancy documents, the Friends of Five Wounds Trail's own history, and local journalism. Each event and figure links to its source. Sister pages cover the Coyote Creek Trail, which this trail would join at the trestle, and the Los Gatos Creek Trail.

        It's an independent project and is not affiliated with the City of San José, VTA or the Friends of Five Wounds Trail.

        • City GIS lists most of the corridor as private land; City memos say VTA owns it and is transferring it.
        • Official lengths differ: 2.17 miles (City), 1.5–1.9 miles in various study and grant areas. This site uses the City GIS reaches, 2.3 miles in all.
        • The 22nd St segment's opening year (about 2006) isn't documented exactly.
        • The gap-cost range is our extrapolation, not an official estimate.
