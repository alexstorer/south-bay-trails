Almaden Valley, San José · Updated

Two creeks, one lake,
and half a trail to Quicksilver

Los Alamitos and Guadalupe creeks meet at Almaden Lake to form the Guadalupe River. A paved trail runs four miles up Los Alamitos Creek on old flood levees and a railroad grade. Guadalupe Creek got a gravel trail to Singletree Way in 2008–09; the rest of a 2004 plan to reach Almaden Quicksilver is waiting on bridges, money and a landfill that won't close until the 2040s.

Meet the trail

A finished trail and a stalled one

In 1967 the County and City proposed a chain of creekside parks instead of a concrete flood channel. The water district built its levees to fit, and a 1987 plan put trails on them. Los Alamitos Creek Trail was paved from Winfield Blvd to Harry Rd by 2007; we couldn't find when.

About 5.9 miles are open: 4.2 paved on Los Alamitos Creek and 1.8 of gravel levee road on Guadalupe Creek, from Almaden Expwy past Masson Dam to Singletree Way.

The other 3.1 miles are all on Guadalupe Creek: two creek bridges above Masson Dam, Guadalupe Mines Rd, and a landfill haul road the City can't use until the landfill closes, now expected at the end of 2044. Two crossings, at Meridian Ave and Almaden Expwy, are also unbuilt.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      The paper trail on money here is thin. We found no construction cost for either creek trail, only plan estimates: $3.0M for the Los Alamitos Creek park chain in 1987 and $9.9M for the Guadalupe Creek Trail in 2004. The biggest documented spending nearby went into planning a lake project that was never built.

      Project table with funding sources

      Promises and what happened

      Los Alamitos Creek got its trail, though we found no record of when. On Guadalupe Creek only the 2004 plan's first, gravel phase was built. Valley Water's lake project went from approval to the unfunded list in under three years.

      The 2004 Guadalupe Creek plan's estimate

      Callander Associates priced the whole 3.9-mile trail at $9.9M in 2004 dollars, including design, contingency and inflation. Almost 40% of it was one item: a bridge over Almaden Expwy that the City now calls no longer feasible. The segments still missing add up to $8.62M.

        About this page

        This page brings together the 1967, 1987 and 2004 City plans, old City trail reports and maps, Valley Water's capital plans, board memos and environmental reports, State environmental filings, City GIS data and local journalism. Each event and figure links to its source.

        An independent project, not affiliated with the City of San José, Valley Water, Santa Clara County Parks or the Bay Area Ridge Trail Council. Part of South Bay Trails.

        • City GIS has no opening dates for these trails. We found no record of when Los Alamitos Creek was paved; the timeline map shows it from mid-2006, the earliest record of the whole trail, though parts are probably older. Guadalupe Creek's dates come from 2008 and 2009 City documents; the short private path GUC07B has no date and stays grey on the timeline map.
        • No construction cost has been found for either trail. The cost chart shows only Valley Water's documented spending on the Almaden Lake project and a 1982 park budget.
        • The 2004 estimate covers 3.9 plan miles; today's GIS maps 4.84 miles of Guadalupe Creek reaches. Per-mile figures for the plans divide by GIS miles where noted.
        • Valley Water's January 2023 decision says construction estimates "nearly tripled" but gives no new figure.
        • The gap-cost range is our extrapolation, in mixed-year dollars, not an official estimate.
