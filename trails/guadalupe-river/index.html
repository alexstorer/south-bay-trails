Alviso to Almaden, San José · Updated

The trail along
the Guadalupe

San José was founded on the Guadalupe River and spent most of its history turning its back on it. Hundreds of millions of dollars of flood control later, a paved trail runs 12 miles from the Bay into downtown, carrying 433,000 trips a year. Then, just past Interstate 280, it stops.

Meet the trail

The river San José was built on

The Guadalupe River Trail follows the river from the salt marshes at Alviso past the tech campuses of North San José, the airport, Guadalupe Gardens and the downtown river park. A separate stretch runs from Chynoweth Ave to Almaden Lake. Part of it is a National Recreation Trail.

About 12.4 miles are continuous from Alviso to Virginia St, plus 1.6 miles at the south end. In 2025 the City counted 433,000 trips.

About 5.3 miles are missing through Willow Glen and South San José. The trail is waiting on Valley Water and the Army Corps to finish rebuilding that stretch of river, a project once due in 2016 and now scheduled for 2033.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      The Guadalupe River Trail rides on hundreds of millions of dollars of flood control. Recent trail spending has gone less to new miles than to clearing encampments, patrolling and reopening access. The missing five miles depend on a flood project the Army Corps has stopped funding.

      Spending table with funding sources

      Promises and what happened

      The upper flood project was supposed to be done by 2016; most of it isn't built, and the finish date is now 2033. The trail gap waits on it.

      The City's estimate for the gap, segment by segment

      The master plan for Virginia St to Chynoweth Ave priced 4.9 miles of trail and three bridges at $54.9 million in its cost table. The most expensive piece, Almaden Expwy to Foxworthy Ave, is $12.9M on its own.
