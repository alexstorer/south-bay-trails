The trail along
Silver Creek

Valley Water spent about $140 million rebuilding Lower Silver Creek, and left levee roads along most of it. San José adopted a plan in 2007 to turn them into a trail from Coyote Creek to Lake Cunningham. Nineteen years later, less than two miles are open.

Meet the trail

A flood channel waiting to be a trail

Lower Silver Creek flows northwest through East San José, from Lake Cunningham past Capitol Park, Mayfair and Alum Rock Ave to Coyote Creek near Watson Park. Its planned trail would link the Coyote Creek and Five Wounds trails to Lake Cunningham, and a separate stretch follows Upper Silver Creek in Evergreen.

About 1.7 miles are open on Lower Silver Creek, between Dobern Ave and Lake Cunningham, plus 0.9 mile on Upper Silver Creek.

About 4.3 miles are missing, including everything downstream of Jackson Ave. The flood work is finished; what's missing is a bridge over Hwy 101, construction money, and agreement among the City, Valley Water and the Army Corps on permits.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      Almost all the money spent on Lower Silver Creek went to flood protection, not trail. Valley Water rebuilt the channel from 1993 to 2020; the City's trail spending with published figures is a few hundred thousand dollars.

      Project table with funding sources

      Promises and what happened

      Valley Water's contracts ran 3–15% over their awards, and two finished a year or more late. The trail they were meant to carry, adopted in 2007, is still waiting on permits.

      The flood project the trail would ride on

      Valley Water rebuilt Lower Silver Creek in stages from 1993 to 2020, with federal stimulus and state money for the upper reaches. The flood reaches listed below came to more than $100 million in contracts; the 2021 capital plan puts the total for Reaches 4–6 at $102M. The 2007 trail plan was designed to run on the levee roads these projects built.
