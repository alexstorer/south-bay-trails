Alviso, San José · Updated

Under a mile, or seventeen?
It depends whose map

San José's Bay Trail runs from Hwy 237 through Alviso to Coyote Creek. The City's map shows less than a mile of it open. The regional map counts about 17 miles of levee, marina and creek trail. Most of the City's route is waiting on a $512 million levee that is half built.

Meet the trail

Two maps of the same shoreline

The Bay Trail is meant to ring the whole Bay. In San José it crosses the Guadalupe River into Alviso, a village below sea level that flooded in 1983, then heads east past the wastewater plant to Coyote Creek. San José adopted a plan for a 13.3-mile section in 2002.

On the City's own map, 0.96 miles are open: a path along Hwy 237 that a neighboring city owns, and a short piece near Gold St. The other 10.8 miles, including the Gold St river crossing studied since 2010, are planned or in design.

MTC's regional map counts about 17 miles of existing trail in and along San José that the City leaves out: dirt levees on the wildlife refuge, the County's marina park, a Valley Water levee, the Hwy 237 trail and Milpitas's Coyote Creek trail. Both are drawn here; the regional segments appear as side routes. The refuge loop will shrink as salt ponds are opened to the tides.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      Almost all the money spent on San José's shoreline has gone to flood control and marsh restoration, not trail. The Bay Trail rides along: on the old salt-pond levees today, and on the new shoreline levee once it's finished. The City's own reaches have had studies but no construction budget.

      Promises and what happened

      The 2005 study expected San José's short-term gaps closed within five years. Twenty-one years later most remain. The levee was to be done by January 2023 for $177.2M; half is built, and the estimate has passed $500M.

      The 2005 regional estimate

      The Bay Trail gap study priced San José's 14 gaps at $16.1M in 2005 dollars, about $1M a mile, as a planning-level estimate. MTC's 2024 plan replaced figures like these with cost ranges, and put the Gold St crossing alone at $5M–$25M.

        This page brings together the City's and MTC's trail maps, San José council memos, Valley Water and Coastal Conservancy records, the 2005 and 2024 regional gap studies, and local journalism. Each event and figure links to its source.

Some (the refuge loop's west side, the Milpitas trail) lie partly outside the city.

        • The new levee's Reaches 1–3 carry a 16-ft aggregate trail, but neither map shows it yet and we found no opening date.
        • Sources disagree on the levee's original authorization ($177.2M or $194M) and its finish (2035 or 2038), and on the 2003 salt-pond acreage (16,500 or 15,100).
