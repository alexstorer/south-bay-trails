The trail along
Los Gatos Creek

From the foot of Lexington Dam, a path follows Los Gatos Creek down the canyon, past Vasona Lake and the Campbell ponds, into Willow Glen. For ten miles it never stops. Then, about two miles short of downtown San José, it starts to come apart.

Meet the trail

Four cities, one creek, one path (mostly)

Los Gatos Creek rises in the Santa Cruz Mountains, fills Lexington Reservoir, and runs north to meet the Guadalupe River beside SAP Center. Four agencies share the trail beside it: the Town of Los Gatos, County Parks, Campbell and San José. In Campbell, San José's 2018 count found more than 1,000 users a day.

Much of the southern part dates from the late 1970s, and it's finished. Today about 10.2 miles connect without a break, from Lexington Dam north to Meridian Ave in San José (County Parks, measuring from the reservoir, says about 9.5).

North of Meridian, the trail breaks up. About 1.5 miles are still missing, in two places: a stretch of Willow Glen where back yards run to the middle of the creek, and the last half-mile to the Guadalupe River, around Diridon Station, where the creek runs in culverts and the plan depends on Google's paused campus.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      Most of the trail was built decades ago, and what it cost isn't online. These are the recent projects with published numbers: total project costs (design, construction, contingency and staff time) from council reports.

      Project table with contracts and funding sources

      Did they deliver? On time and on budget

      For each recent project, the finish date planned when the contract was awarded, compared with when it actually opened. The Three Creeks bridge was four years late after a lawsuit over the trestle it replaced. The Hwy 9 connector finished a few months late and within its contingency, but its construction bill ran about 20% above the 2022 estimate.

      San José's own estimate, from 1985

      The 1985 master plan priced the whole 3.5-mile San José trail, Bascom Ave to the Guadalupe River, at $2.21 million in 1985 dollars, including contingency and design. The two phases that were never built, Lincoln–Lonus and Park Ave–Guadalupe, were budgeted at under $700,000 between them. Today the City's and VTA's estimates for the downtown pieces alone come to $13.9M.

      Estimate the cost of closing the gaps

      There's no official estimate for the whole job. The Willow Glen gap has never been designed or priced. This calculator applies a cost-per-mile assumption, in today's total project dollars, to the remaining mainline gaps.
