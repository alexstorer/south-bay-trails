Willow Glen, San José · Updated

The trail that connects
two of its three creeks

An old Western Pacific spur through Willow Glen became a trail in pieces: bought in 2011, paved in 2018, bridged in 2021 after a six-year fight over a 1922 trestle. It now links Los Gatos Creek to the Guadalupe River. The third creek, Coyote, is still two miles away on a route nobody has drawn.

Meet the trail

Two creeks down, one to go

The Three Creeks Trail follows the Western Pacific's Willow Glen branch, which carried fruit from the canneries from 1922 until Union Pacific stopped running trains in 2000. San José bought the western mile in 2011, mostly with Open Space Authority and County money, and planned the trail around its railroad history.

About 1.2 miles are open, from the Los Gatos Creek Trail at Lonus St to the Guadalupe River at Minnesota Ave, broken once by a sidewalk detour at Willow St and Bird Ave.

The other 2.2 miles, east across Hwy 87, Monterey Rd and the rail lines to Coyote Creek, are mostly private land. Focus groups in 2008 called a continuous trail there unlikely, and the 2015 vision study's elevated "SKY Lane" has no budget.

      What it has cost, and what's left

      The western mile was bought with grants and built in three pieces. The bridge cost the most, mostly because of the six years it spent in court. The eastern half has no cost estimate at all, because no one has chosen a route.

      Project table with contracts and funding sources

      Promises and what happened

      The bridge was supposed to be built by April 2015, before a state grant ran out. Six years of lawsuits later, its contract had grown 53%. The paving projects ran about six months late each.

      The 2014 master plan's estimate

      The City's master plan priced the whole western trail, Lonus St to the Guadalupe River, at $5.92M in 2013 dollars. More than half of that was contingency, City management and design fees; the trail itself was about $1.1M.

      Estimate the cost of closing the gaps

      Most of what's missing is east of the Guadalupe, on private land and across rail lines and busy roads. This calculator applies a cost-per-mile assumption to the length; an elevated route like SKY Lane would sit at the top of the range or beyond it.
